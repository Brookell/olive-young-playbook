Key takeaways

  1. Demand is real and fast. K-beauty exports to France grew 71.5% in one year.
  2. Paris is not empty. Sephora, Galeries Lafayette, Monoprix and K-beauty shops already sell Korean brands.
  3. Skin diagnosis is not unique. Moida offers it on the Champs-Élysées since summer 2026.
  4. Olive Young’s edge is its own brands and curation, not “K-beauty” in general.
  5. No French presence yet. The Sephora deal covers the US, Asia and the UK (2027), not France.
  6. EU rules matter: safety assessment, CPNP, French labels, EU-approved sunscreen filters.

06 · SWOT

Olive Young in France

SStrengths

  • Described as Korea’s top beauty retailer; revenue +21.8%
  • 12 own brands under its control
  • Curation expertise and trend speed
  • Global Mall already serves 150 countries

WWeaknesses

  • No stores or team in France yet
  • Low brand awareness among French shoppers
  • Stores abroad only since May 2026 (US)

OOpportunities

  • K-beauty exports to France +71.5%
  • Gen Z values gifts and proven results
  • Sephora partnership could later reach France
  • Europe distribution already starting (Poland)

TThreats

  • Moida already offers K-beauty + skin diagnosis in Paris
  • Sephora and Galeries Lafayette expanding K-beauty
  • Strong French pharmacy brands
  • EU compliance cost, especially sunscreens
07 · For our presentation

Five ways to sharpen our pitch

1

Lead with Olive Young’s own brands

Our real uniqueness. We found none of them in the French stores above. “K-beauty” alone is everywhere.

2

Know Moida, our closest competitor

Our difference: own brands, a French routine finder and a refill loop.

3

Position the kit as an “upgrade”

French Gen Z already use ~8 products. Offer a K-beauty swap, plus a gift.

4

Put compliance in months 1–3

CPNP notification, French labels and an EU-compliant sunscreen.

5

Be ready for “why not Sephora?”

The deal doesn’t include France today, and our own lab builds Olive Young’s brand, not Sephora’s.
